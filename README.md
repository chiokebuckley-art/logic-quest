v0.13.0 adds the Kid Journey, from the Logic Quest Kid Journey brief (October 13, 2026): every lesson sits in one of nine grade bands (Grade 1 to Grade 8, then high school), each a few worlds of rounds on a star sky. A new player needs only a name, then a grade or a short untimed placement check. Rounds earn gold or silver stars (never from a Next tap), three stars give Pip a sticker, a world check with every answer right builds the world's building, and a band's last world check earns the grade badge with a printable certificate; a missed check locks until the missed rounds are played again; the flame is now days this week. Grades 1 to 3 read like a picture book: every screen of their 23 rounds fits a word budget counted from the rendered text (12, 20 and 30 words), with a picture first on every card, boards marked one mark at a time, picture or yes/no tiles, pictogram scenes in place of clue lists, a one-line Not yet with Show me and Try again, a hint of eight words or a picture, a Ready? Go before every check, no timer, and read-aloud that starts by itself in Grades 1 and 2. Where a classic question's answers are whole sentences, kid mode asks a kid-only twin of the same skill checked by tests; the classic lessons, ids, answers and pass rules are unchanged. Grades 4 to 8 read shorter too: the wordiest cards of Arguments, Guess the rule, Ways to Think, the sign lessons, Spot the Trick and Knights and Knaves lost a third to two thirds of their body text, with every idea, defined word and example kept. The spec is `source/docs/KID-JOURNEY.md`. The Grade 1 NOT round shows NOT on cards: every card, question and board asks about a drawn set of cards ("NOT all red?" over two red and one blue). Built from `command-center` branch `ccr-4bce2062-2v9qj3`, commit `e5a5851`.

v0.11.0 makes the Journey a first-principles chain, from the Logic Quest Curriculum Review (October 11, 2026): every idea is defined before it is used, and every stop is built. A new stop, Arguments, sits right after True or False? (shown as Stop 2; later stops' shown numbers move up by one, while ids and saves stay as they were): givens and the claim, does it follow (Must, Might, Can't), follows even when silly, solid arguments; it names premise, conclusion, argument, valid and sound. Six stops are built: All, Some, None (rings; “some” is true when all are; “not all” means “some are not”; the empty ring), Truth Tables (rows are every case; the IF table; same meaning when the columns match; always true, never true), Circuit Lab (in a line is AND, side by side is OR, the flipper is NOT, two circuits with the same table), Proof (four ways to prove; every step cites; cover every case; suppose and crash; one counterexample is enough), and on the side track Spot the Trick (flip tricks, the middle trick and the swap, going in circles and only two doors, too few and everybody says) and Clues & Chances (likely not certain, each draw is new, out of 100, would I see this clue if I were wrong). If… then gains Only if, and unless; Both ways: needed and enough; and names converse, inverse and contrapositive. Every stop check ends with a why item, every stop after the first opens with a warm-up from the earlier idea, a Words tab in the Library collects each term once its lesson is done, and the Observatory rings open after the stops they build on. The spec is `source/docs/CURRICULUM-PLAN.md`. Built from `command-center` branch `ccr-4bce2062-2v9qj3`, commit `1b3b822`.

v0.10.0 adds the Pattern Observatory, from the handoff *Pattern Recognition from First Principles* (v2): a side constellation of four rings (Stops 14–17) that opens from the start and never blocks the main track. Ring 1 First Lights (Star Chain, Repair Bench, Echo Bells), Ring 2 Rule Rise (Growing Staircase, Rule Machine, Clock Tower), Ring 3 Deep Sky (Mirror Pool, Matrix Gate, Analogy Bridge), Ring 4 Proof Lantern (proved, a guess, or false; counterexamples and separating tests; why a stated construction guarantees its pattern). One routine on every screen (Notice, Describe, Compare, Test, Predict, Explain), five phases per place (See, Explain, Do, Transfer, Review), a number pad instead of the tablet keyboard, help that is never grading (a repaired item counts as supported, a fresh twin as independent, first tries never change), an evidence profile per place (Practiced, Lesson complete, Independent, Transferred, Retained), a short untimed diagnostic that sets a level per track and picks each place's quiz at that level, places that open on skills with three-question primers, and plain labels for grown-ups. The Pattern Lab bridges vary their blocks, Twin Isles has its own content, bridges record first tries, and with the owner's OK the bridges and the shared chip use the same routine order. The spec is `source/docs/OBSERVATORY.md`. Built from `command-center` branch `ccr-4bce2062-2v9qj3`, commit `960bc4d`, which includes all of v0.9.0.

v0.9.0 teaches the hidden distinction: a learner can know every word in a question and still be confused because two ideas have collapsed into one (the treasure signs showed it: “the treasure is in this chest” read as “this chest's sign is true”). Every lesson was audited for that kind of gap (`source/docs/audit/hidden-distinctions.md`) and every serious one fixed: a side-by-side card and a tiny board before the first real puzzle, the reason in words under every mark, steps that fade, a mix-up named as a mix-up, and an “I'm confused” button that finds the mixed-up idea without giving the answer. The design rule for every app is `source/docs/design-rule-hidden-distinctions.md`.

v0.4.1 teaches before the quiz (the skill-drill handoff). Every lesson in Stops 1–6 is See, Do, Quiz: the key ideas end on one case already marked; before any quiz the learner marks a new case on that same board by taps (true or false, fits or not, a check or a cross, a count, keep or reject), and "Check my marks" names the first wrong mark; the quiz then uses only the rule the lesson taught. Treasure signs keeps the chest example (it still concludes Silver), adds Mark a case (Silver shown, Gold marked by the learner), and starts its quiz with the frozen Ice, Fire and Moss caves, all "Exactly one sign is true". The other sign rules now have their own lessons in Stop 1: "Every sign is false", "Exactly two signs are true" and "The owner's sign". A lesson passes only when its boards are marked right and 3 answers are right on the first try with no hint; tapping Next through the cards never passes, and lessons open in order. Each stop's drill worksheet is in `source/docs/audit/drill-stopN.md`. Built from `command-center` branch `ccr-4bce2062-2v9qj3`, commit `77bd33e`, which includes all of v0.4.0.

v0.4.0 reorganises the app around one page per stop. The tabs are Home · Journey · Arcade · Library · Me. Home shows the one next thing; each stop's lessons, checks, practice, Pattern Lab events and repair cards live on its own Stop page; the Library indexes every idea and puzzle; Find anything searches all of it; and the colours are brighter, one meaning each (the same palette as the Engineering Quest remake). Saves carry over unchanged.

v0.3.1 makes every wrong answer teach first. After a miss, the explanation shows what the answer means, exactly where it fails (with a worked counterexample), why the right answer works, and a short Remember. Then comes "Try this question again", then new examples on the same idea, answered without help. Grown-ups see this help counted apart from first-try answers. The reference is Stop 1, Lesson 3 (The NOT flip). Each stop's audit worksheet is in `source/docs/audit/`. Built from `command-center` branch `ccr-4bce2062-2v9qj3`, commit `c7899b49651d060ac7e2b9c0eabd3905e64ae06a`, which includes all of v0.3.0.
